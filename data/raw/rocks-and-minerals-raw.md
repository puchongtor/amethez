## EXTRA DETAIL — IMPACTITES / TEKTITES / METEORITES / GLASS / ORGANICS

### Meteorites (ROCKS | METEORITES, ~p.74–75) — imgs 067–068
**Definition:** Rocks formed on other Solar System bodies that collided with Earth. Friction may vaporize many as meteors; survivors = meteorites. Not classified as igneous/metamorphic/sedimentary in the usual sense. Several tonnes reach Earth yearly (ongoing accretion).

**Three classes:**
1. **Iron meteorites** — mostly Fe–Ni metals **kamacite** + **taenite** (rare on Earth); minor troilite (FeS), silicates (olivine, pyroxene), phosphides, graphite. Origin: cores of large asteroids. Etched slabs show **Widmanstätten pattern** (slow cooling). Specimen example: Namibia.
2. **Stony-iron** — mix of metal + silicates. **Pallasites** = cm-sized translucent olivine (rarely pyroxene) in Fe–Ni matrix (~1 olivine : 2 metal). Origin: core–mantle boundary of differentiated asteroids. Example locality: Atacama Desert, Chile (regmaglypts / “thumb-print” hollows from atmospheric ablation).
3. **Stony meteorites** — **chondrites** (contain silicate spheres **chondrules**, usually ≤1 mm) vs **achondrites** (geologically processed).  
   - Properties (stony): Rock type igneous/metamorphic/sedimentary; Fossils none macroscopic; Major mins olivine, pyroxene, plagioclase; Colour grey/greenish/tan/black; Texture fine–medium.  
   - **Martian SNCs:** Shergottites, Nakhlites, Chassignites; **ALH84001** noted for debated microfossil-like tubules.

**Impact features:** Barringer (Meteor) Crater, Arizona — 1.2 km diameter; ~50 m meteorite ~50,000 years ago; nearly vaporized. Asteroid Ida + moon Dactyl illustrated.

### Tektites & Moldavite (~p.75) — img 068 ★ SPECIAL
- **Tektites:** Terrestrial rock melted and ejected by large meteorite impact, quenched to glass. Size typically mm–cm.
- **Shapes shown:** cone-in-cone; disc/button; dumbbell; irregular.
- **Moldavite:** Beautiful green glass tektite from **Czech Republic**, linked to **Ries Crater, Germany**. Book shows raw pitted bottle-green specimen + **moldavite pendant** mounted with a topaz crystal.
- **Other impactites:** **Suevite** (impact breccia); **shatter cones** (pressure waves; nose points toward crater).
- **Not found in this set:** dedicated Libyan Desert Glass / Muong Nong / Australite locality cards; Thai tektite; fulgurite.

### Obsidian (IGNEOUS ROCKS, p.43) — img 039 ★ SPECIAL
- Natural volcanic glass; lava cools too fast for crystals. Chemistry usually rhyolite-like.
- **Rock props:** Felsic volcanic igneous; Major: glass; Minor: hematite, feldspar; Colour black/brown/red; Texture amorphous.
- Fracture **conchoidal**; vitreous lustre; slightly harder than window glass → razor edges (tools, Aztec mirrors/knives, Olmec mask, Fabergé green obsidian horse).
- **Varieties:** red/brown (hematite, flow-banding); **snowflake** (white feldspar/quartz spherulites from devitrification); **Apache tear** (small polished nodules); pitchstone (resinous from absorbed water); golden sheen from gas bubbles.
- Localities: Newberry Crater OR; Mt Hekla Iceland; Eolie Islands Italy; Obsidian Cliff, Yellowstone USA.

### Amber (ORGANIC GEMS, p.314–315+) — imgs 302–304 ★ SPECIAL
- Fossilized resin of extinct conifers; often with lignite. Younger resin = **copalite/copal**.
- Greek *elektron* → “electricity” (triboelectric charge when rubbed).
- **Props:** Group Organics–hydrocarbons; Crystal system none; Comp. hydrocarbon (approx. C₁₀H₁₆O); Habit amorphous; H 2–2½; Cleavage none; Fracture conchoidal; Colour yellow / brownish / reddish; Streak white; Lustre resinous; SG 1.1; Transparent–translucent; RI 1.54–1.55.
- Baltic coast (Denmark, Sweden, Gdańsk) ~90% world supply; insects/spiders trapped 40–60 Ma; Bronze Age cup; **Amber Room** (Catherine Palace; WWII loss; restored). “Sun-spangled” beads from water/heat fractures.

### Jet, Coral, Pearl (Organic Gems, imgs 305–311)
- **Jet:** compact organic (lignite-related); Raton NM; Native American jewellery; often with opal/pearl inlays.
- **Coral:** colonial polyps; reefs; used as organic gem.
- **Pearl:** molluscs; natural scarce → cultured pearls feature (p.324).

## MINERAL DIRECTORY (scientific cards — key data)

### NATIVE ELEMENTS (~p.112–125)
- **Gold:** Native metal; malleable; inert; crystals rare (octahedral/dodecahedral); grains/scales/dendrites; hydrothermal veins with quartz+pyrite; placers; Witwatersrand conglomerates; Cripple Creek tellurides; California/Australia rushes. Soft in pure form → alloys for jewellery.
- **Silver:** Companion coinage metal; Aspen CO veins; sterling 92.5% Ag + Cu; Anatolia mines from ~4000 BC.
- **Platinum:** Recognized 1735; placer flakes/nuggets; Pinto Colombia; Bushveld association.
- **Copper:** Native metal; historical artifacts; oxidized to azurite/malachite.
- **Sulphur:** Native nonmetal; fumarole crusts (e.g. Kawah Ijen); yellow; abundant in sulphides.
- **Arsenic:** Native semimetal; Saxony localities; linked to orpiment.
- **Diamond / Hope Diamond (p.124):** Pure C; H 10; adamantine lustre; perfect cleavage; high RI/dispersion. Hope: 45.52 ct fancy blue, asymmetrical cushion antique brilliant (~58+ facets); Golconda/Kollur origin via Tavernier → French Blue → Hope → Cartier/McLean → Harry Winston → Smithsonian 1958. Kimberlite pipes (Kimberley SA) host source rock.

### SULPHIDES / SULPHOSALTS (selected)
- **Galena:** PbS; cubic; major Pb ore; Coeur d’Alene ID.
- **Pentlandite:** (Ni,Fe)₉S₈; massive/granular; Ni ore; also in meteorites; Kola.
- **Chalcocite:** Cu₂S; important Cu ore; prismatic/tabular/pseudohexagonal twins.
- **Cinnabar:** HgS; Hunan China finest; pigment/lacquer.
- **Realgar:** AsS; prismatic/granular; Freiberg.
- **Stibnite:** Sb₂S₃; principal Sb ore; striated prisms; Hunan.
- **Cobaltite:** CoAsS; 1832 name; Broken Hill.
- **Arsenopyrite:** FeAsS; most common As mineral (=mispickel).
- **Pyrite / Marcasite:** FeS₂ polymorphs — pyrite cubic, marcasite orthorhombic (book intro).
- **Stephanite:** Ag₅SbS₄; “brittle silver ore”; Saxony.
- **Pyrargyrite:** Ag₃SbS₃; “dark ruby silver”; deepens on light.
- **Boulangerite:** Pb₅Sb₄S₁₁; flexible needles; hydrothermal.

### OXIDES (selected gems & ores)
- **Corundum / Sapphire:** Al₂O₃; Hex–trigonal; H 9; SG 4.0–4.1; RI 1.76–1.77; adamantine–vitreous; barrel/prismatic. Colours most hues; Cr → pink–ruby continuum; Fe common. Heat/irradiation treatments. Sources: Myanmar, Sri Lanka, Nigeria, Thailand, Australia, Brazil, Kashmir, Montana placers, etc. Industrial abrasive + emery (+magnetite). Star sapphire (rutile asterism). Logan Sapphire 423 ct (Sri Lanka) noted.
- **Ruby:** Red Cr-bearing corundum; Cowee Creek NC; star rubies; Myanmar/Sri Lanka gravels; often confused historically with red spinel.
- **Spinel:** Historic “Timur Ruby” & “Black Prince’s Ruby” are spinels in Crown Jewels.
- **Cuprite:** Cu₂O; cubic; “plush copper” fibrous variety; Cornwall.
- **Zincite:** ZnO; red oxide of zinc.
- **Franklinite:** (Zn,Mn,Fe)(Fe,Mn)₂O₄; spinel group; Franklin/Sterling Hill NJ.
- **Cassiterite:** SnO₂; wood tin / stream tin; Myanmar.
- **Anatase / Rutile / Brookite:** TiO₂ polymorphs; Swiss Alps anatase.
- **Hematite:** Fe₂O₃; colouring agent in rocks/obsidian; oxide section coverage.
- **Fluorescent minerals (p.160):** Fluorite (often violet-blue; Er traces); manganoan calcite scarlet-pink; scapolite yellow (variable).

### HYDROXIDES / HALIDES
- **Brucite:** Mg(OH)₂; Urals.
- **Fluorite:** CaF₂; cubic; H 4; perfect octahedral cleavage; widest colour range; veins with Pb–Ag ores; pegmatites; hot springs. **Blue John** banded purple/white(/yellow) fluorite, Castleton Derbyshire (Roman vessels; Matthew Boulton ornaments). Fluoresces.
- **Halite / rock salt:** evaporite sedimentary rock + mineral; WWII salt-mine art storage.

### CARBONATES
- **Calcite / Aragonite / Dolomite:** rock-forming; cave formations (stalactites/mites); limestone/chalk.
- **Malachite p.184:** Cu₂CO₃(OH)₂; monoclinic; bright green; botryoidal/banded; H 3½–4; perfect cleavage; SG 3.9–4.0; adamantine–silky; pale green streak. Altered Cu deposits ± azurite. Kolwezi DRC; Urals (51 t masses); Bisbee; Morocco; China; Australia. Earliest Cu ore (~4000 BC); pigment; eye paint; ornamental. Russian Malachite feature p.186.
- **Azurite / Chrysocolla:** associated Cu secondaries (mine photos with malachite).
- **Rhodochrosite:** cover specimen; Mn carbonate (group coverage in carbonates).

### PHOSPHATES / ARSENATES / VANADATES
- **Torbernite p.191:** Cu uranyl phosphate hydrate; tetragonal; bright green tabular; H 2–2½; SG 3.2; radioactive — handle carefully; Cornwall.
- **Erythrite (“cobalt bloom”):** Co₃(AsO₄)₂·8H₂O; monoclinic; purple-pink acicular; H 1½–2½; SG 3.1; Bou Azzer Morocco; Ontario.
- **Variscite:** AlPO₄·2H₂O; orthorhombic; apple-green cryptocrystalline; H 4½; SG 2.6; RI 1.6–1.7; gem/ornamental; can stain with oils; Bisbee AZ; mistaken for turquoise.
- **Turquoise:** Cu–Al phosphate hydrate; Mesoamerican Turquoise feature p.198; jewellery with gold/lapis/carnelian (Tutankhamun).

### SULPHATES etc.
- **Gypsum:** CaSO₄·2H₂O; monoclinic; H 2; SG 2.3; RI 1.52–1.53; subvitreous–pearly. Varieties: **selenite**, **satin spar**, **alabaster**, desert roses. White Sands NM; Cave of Swords Naica MX (>2 m selenite); Lake Eyre AU.
- **Scheelite:** CaWO₄; Bohemia/Czech; tungstate.
- **Brochantite / Melanterite / Cyanotrichite:** secondary Cu/Fe sulphates; Tsumeb, Nevada, Urals.

### SILICATES — FELDSPARS & FELDSPATHOIDS
- **Orthoclase / Moonstone:** K-feldspar; Goodsprings NV; moonstone schiller (adularescence).
- **Microcline / Amazonite:** Urals; green microcline variety.
- **Albite:** Na end-member plagioclase; tabular twins; rock-forming.
- **Andesine:** Andes Mts namesake.
- **Labradorite p.241:** (Na,Ca)Al₁–₂Si₃–₂O₈; triclinic; blue/grey/white; usually massive; H 6–6½; perfect cleavage; SG 2.7; RI 1.56–1.57. **Schiller** from exsolution lamellae; gem **spectrolite** (Finland); Oregon “sunstone” = labradorite. Localities: Madagascar, Finland, Russia, India, Mexico, USA. In basalt/gabbro/diorite/andesite/amphibolite.
- **Lapis lazuli / Lazurite:** Badakhshan Afghanistan historic mines; Standard of Ur jewellery; ultramarine pigment history.
- **Haüyne / Sodalite group / Leucite / Nepheline:** feldspathoids; leucite fertilizer (Italy).

### ZEOLITES / PHYLLOSILICATES (selected)
- Heulandite, Mesolite, Stilbite, Gmelinite, Analcime family — open hydrated frameworks; India (Pune/Aurangabad) famous.
- **Scapolite / Pollucite / Petalite:** Li/Cs collectors’ stones; petalite Minas Gerais.
- **Muscovite / Biotite / Lepidolite:** micas; biotite “black mica” pegmatites (Pike’s Peak, Bancroft…); lepidolite Li mica lilac, with beryl/topaz/tourmaline/spodumene; Dixon NM.
- **Talc:** softest Mohs reference; Urals.
- **Chrysotile / Serpentine:** asbestos; peridotite veins.
- **Kaolinite / Clay minerals / Vermiculite / Chlorite / Clinochlore / Chamosite / Prehnite.**

### INOSILICATES / CYCLOSILICATES / NESOSILICATES
- **Diopside / Enstatite / Jadeite / Nephrite:** pyroxenes & amphiboles; jadeite Myanmar; nephrite Kobuk River AK + worldwide; Chinese Han burial suits (gold/silver/bronze wire by rank; Prince Liu Sheng / Princess Tou Wan ~2156 plaques).
- **Rhodonite:** Mn inosilicate; rose colour.
- **Riebeckite / Crocidolite:** blue asbestos; tiger’s-eye precursor.
- **Tourmaline group:** complex borosilicates; species elbaite, dravite, schorl, liddicoatite; varieties indicolite, achroite, rubellite, verdelite, watermelon. Prismatic, dichroic, colour-zoned, piezoelectric. Pegmatites (Pala CA; Mount Mica ME). Schorl black Fe-rich; dravite brown Mg-rich.
- **Beryl group:** Be₃Al₂Si₆O₁₈; hexagonal; H 7½–8; SG 2.6–3.0; RI 1.57–1.60.
  - **Aquamarine** (Fe blue-green) — Urals
  - **Morganite** (Mn pink) — Minas Gerais
  - **Emerald** (Cr/V green) p.292 — Muzo/Coscuez Colombia; Urals mica schist; trapiche; Zambia/Pakistan/Brazil…
  - Also goshenite, heliodor
- **Cordierite (iolite):** dichroic blue.
- **Garnet group (~p.300):** cubic nesosilicates; species include almandine, spessartine, andradite, pyrope, grossular, uvarovite (series).
- **Zircon:** ZrSiO₄; Myanmar gem gravels; heat-treated colours; high RI/fire.
- **Topaz:** Al₂SiO₄(F,OH)₂; Brazilian Minas Gerais classic; prismatic.
- **Titanite (sphene):** superb fire when faceted.
- **Andalusite / Kyanite / Sillimanite / Staurolite:** Al₂SiO₅ polymorphs + Fe Al silicate twins (Taos cruciform staurolite); Bahia gem kyanite.
- **Olivine / Peridot:** (Mg,Fe)₂SiO₄; gem peridot; Hawaiian olivine sands; mantle mineral; also in pallasites/meteorites.
- **Epidote / Clinozoisite / Axinite / Vesuvianite / Datolite / Humite.**

### ROCKS (brief — non-mineral encyclopedia use)
- **Igneous:** Granite, pegmatite, granodiorite, anorthosite, dolerite, gabbro/peridotite, kimberlite, basalt, rhyolite, phonolite/trachyte, tuff, pumice, **obsidian**.
- **Sedimentary:** Limestone, chalk, rock salt, rock gypsum, shale, sandstone, greensand, arkose, flint/chert, conglomerate; nodules/concretions (pyrite “suns”).
- **Metamorphic:** Marble, slate, schist, amphibolite, granulite, **quartzite**, gneiss.
